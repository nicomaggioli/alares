Three catches that kept projects moving.

Most of what goes wrong on a project is not dramatic. It is a detail that nobody looked at until it was in the way. Here are three from our own work, and what happened because someone looked first.

Albany: the chimney stack

Alares is the prime designer on the boiler plant replacement at the VA Albany Medical Center. The project called for a lot of investigation of the existing plant, and steady contact with the VA engineering team to confirm every local and federal requirement was met.

That is how the chimney stack came up. The boiler plant stack was not meeting air quality requirements. We identified the problem and corrected it, and it was resolved without a change order.

East Orange: the footing and the tunnel

At the East Orange VA, the boiler plant needed seismic upgrades, including new walls to brace the building. The footing for the south wall landed right next to the underground tunnel that connects the plant to the main hospital.

We made a site visit, worked through it with the contractor, and developed a footing detail that cleared the tunnel. The hospital kept operating the whole time.

Rock Island: the old gun range

At Rock Island, Illinois, the VA was acquiring about 9.7 acres from the Arsenal to expand the national cemetery. The site history showed that part of the parcel had been a gun range.

A records review was not enough for land with that history, so the due diligence included a Phase II environmental site assessment, which means actually sampling the ground. The VA got its answer before it took ownership of the land.

What they have in common

None of these was found by luck. Each one came out of investigating the existing conditions and asking questions before committing to a plan. Problems are cheaper the earlier you look for them, and the looking is part of the job.
